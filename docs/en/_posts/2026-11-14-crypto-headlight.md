## The week in three sentences

A fallen chip king, nervous investors and the question of whom we can actually trust with our digital assets: this week offered plenty of reasons for a bad mood. At the same time, crypto technology is moving into shop checkouts, bank vaults and the heart of traditional securities trading. Your Dude takes a look at both sides with you — because there can be a world of difference between a falling price and a failing idea.

<h2 id="trust">4. Whom do we trust with our crypto?</h2>

The question of trust goes beyond choosing between an exchange and a hardware wallet. Retailers, employees, software and the procedures used to authorise transfers matter too.

**Binance: progress in freezing the stolen funds.** According to the FBI, North Korean attackers stole **more than USD 6.00 billion** from Binance around **29 October**. The group is identified as “TraderTraitor”. On **12 November**, the US Department of Justice said authorities and companies had jointly frozen **more than USD 2.70 billion**; **more than USD 1.20 billion of that amount** was covered by judicial seizure or restraint orders. That is progress, but it is not yet repayment to victims. These amounts overlap and must not be added together. [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/north-korea-responsible-for-6-billion-binance-attack) · [US Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency)

**Trezor: three tampered devices confirmed.** The manufacturer identified three Safe 5 devices supplied through CryptoBilis and purchased between **20 August and 23 September**. In its 12 November report, it said the affected owners had reported no financial losses. An update released on 11 November blocks the implants it found; it cannot make recovery words secret again once they have been exposed. Trezor also reduced its reseller network by around **80.00%**. The attack targeted the supply chain leading to the buyer. [Trezor](https://trezor.io/blog/news/our-cryptobilis-investigation-what-we-found-and-what-weve-changed)

**FaceStealer: the familiar face on a video call may be fake.** The US Department of Justice attributes thefts of **more than USD 400.00 million over 18 months** to this separate North Korean campaign. Attackers impersonate known contacts, then claim there is a technical problem. Anyone who runs the suggested “repair” installs malware. The target is trust between people. [US Department of Justice, 12 November](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-actions-disrupt-north-koreas-massive-theft-cryptocurrency)

On **12 November**, South Korea's regulator responded with tougher security requirements and plans for greater scope to suspend deposits and withdrawals preventively when a hack is suspected. This exposes a real tension: **a freeze can protect assets while also denying their owners access.** [South Korean financial regulator](https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010107/88133)

Against this backdrop, **Deutsche Bank is launching its custody service** for corporate and institutional clients in Europe, with BaFin approval according to its announcement. Its initial coverage includes Bitcoin, Ether, USDC, EURC and EURAU. The bank describes segregated responsibilities, multiple approvals and offline storage. Another service is becoming available to professional clients. Security remains a matter of verifiable procedures; a big name alone does not settle it. [Deutsche Bank, November 2026](https://corporates.db.com/more/latest-news/deutsche-bank-launches-digital-asset-custody-solution-for-institutional-and-corporate-clients)

<h2 id="europe">5. Europe tightens its grip</h2>

**The digital dollar comes under partial European supervision.** Since **12 November**, the European Banking Authority (EBA) has assumed specific responsibilities for Circle, the European USDC issuer: capital, reserves, liquidity, and recovery and redemption plans. Other duties remain with France's ACPR. What is new is the effective transfer of responsibilities announced in October — USDC was not previously unsupervised. [EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-classifies-usdc-first-significant-e-money-token-european-union)

A newly published **ECB study** adds a societal perspective: **5.00%** of respondents reported holding stablecoins, representing more than one third of respondents who held crypto. Dollar stablecoins dominated. The surveys were conducted in **August and November 2025**. They show adoption at that time, not a newly measured growth rate for this week. [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/focus/2026/html/ecb.ebbox202607_08~f4ddf0117c.nl.html)

**At Binance, regulation becomes an account issue.** Affected European accounts are due to close on **31 December 2026**. CoinDesk obtained confirmation from a spokesperson on **7 November**, with further reporting following this week. Reports identify affected users in France, Spain, Italy and Poland. This does not imply the closure of all EU accounts. The background is the lack of MiCA authorisation. [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/11/07/binance-users-in-some-european-countries-told-to-pull-crypto-before-fees-kick-in-with-accounts-closing-by-year-end)

**In Germany, there is fresh hope for the holding-period exemption.** On 12 November, Bitcoin2Go cited a BILD report drawing on coalition sources that the proposed new crypto tax would not be introduced in 2027. That goes beyond a simple scheduling delay, but remains an attributed political report. The planned abolition should not therefore be treated as definitively abandoned. For comparable private disposals of crypto assets, existing law continues to allow tax-free sales after more than one year. [Bitcoin2Go, 12 November](https://bitcoin-2go.de/news/krypto-haltefrist-koennte-bleiben)

<h2 id="politics">6. The political weather</h2>

The announcement came on Friday. Markets will be interested in trade, international conflicts and possible agreements; the outcome of the meeting is still unknown. For data centres, mining and digital infrastructure, the long-term question is practical: where is reliable, affordable energy available? We do not infer an immediate price effect on our indices. [Reuters, 13 November](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/conflicting-demands-cop31-2026-11-13/)

**Germany is debating its fiscal limits.** On **11 November**, the German Council of Economic Experts presented its annual report, “Fiscal policy facing hard choices” (original German title: “Finanzpolitik vor harten Entscheidungen”). The dispute over debt, priorities and relief measures also forms the backdrop to the tax debate. The Bundestag's Budget Committee completed its final adjustments to the 2027 budget; this is distinct from final parliamentary passage into law. [Council of Economic Experts](https://www.sachverstaendigenrat-wirtschaft.de/publikationen/jahresgutachten.html) · [Bundestag](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw46-pa-haushalt-1231890)

<h2 id="wall-street">7. When crypto exchanges become Wall Street</h2>

**Hyperliquid is extending the use of its trading infrastructure.** An analysis published on 10 November using Glassnode data put the seven-day average volume of equity-linked contracts at **126.00% of the platform's Bitcoin-contract volume**. This is a ratio of traded notional values, not a count of new shareholders or an inflow of the same amount. We use the precise ratio as a reported figure. [Analysis](https://coinedition.com/hyperliquid-stock-perpetuals-top-bitcoin-volume-again-as-traders-eye-equities/)

The technology allows external providers to create their own markets for futures-style contracts with no fixed expiry. **A contract linked to a share is not a share:** it does not confer ordinary ownership of the company or voting rights. Productive use and speculation are not mutually exclusive here — the infrastructure is being used to trade price risk. [Hyperliquid documentation](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals)

The distinction is just as important for the **Anthropic IPO**. Through xStocks, Kraken lets eligible clients register interest in an allocation; it is not guaranteed and, according to the provider, a **5% spread** is added to the offer price. OKX instead offers a leveraged pre-IPO price contract **without ownership rights in Anthropic**. “Buy before the IPO” describes two very different products here. [Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/in/articles/anthropic-ipo-access) · [OKX](https://www.okx.com/help/voucher-anthropic-x-perp)

On the other side, **MSCI is drawing a line around Bitcoin treasury companies**: Strategy and Metaplanet are losing membership of the relevant indices. The November changes take effect at the close on **30 November**. Funds tracking these indices must adjust their holdings accordingly. The immediate effect concerns company shares — it does not automatically mean selling the Bitcoin on their balance sheets. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/msci-drop-strategy-global-indexes-putting-billions-passive-fund-inflows-risk-2026-11-11/) · [MSCI](https://ir.msci.com/node/23051/pdf)

New York set out a related expectation for crypto advertising on 12 November: **“fee-free” must not hide costs through spreads or mark-ups.** Verifiable claims and clear descriptions of risk are part of the product, just as much as its technology. [NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/il20261110-guidance-on-advertising-and-marketing)

<h2 id="good-news">9. The good news to finish: crypto reaches the checkout</h2>

**Square brings Bitcoin payments to eligible US merchants.** Customers pay through Lightning; merchants can receive settlement in dollars. Square's documentation confirms the available process and automatic activation for eligible new US locations. Media reports put completion of the rollout on **10 November**. The often-cited figure of more than four million businesses describes Square's network size; it does not tell us how many actually accept and process Bitcoin payments. [Square](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8622-accept-and-manage-bitcoin-payments) · [Rollout report](https://mabonchain.com/news/square-completes-bitcoin-payments-rollout-4-million-merchants/)

**WORPT makes stablecoins usable for visitors to Thailand.** Unveiled on **13 November**, the app connects conversion of supported stablecoins into baht with the local QR payment network. Merchants receive baht and do not need a crypto wallet. The service operates within the Tourist-DigiPay pilot programme. Its practical appeal is that it connects existing crypto holdings to a payment method already accepted locally. Everyday use will determine whether this results in cheaper, widely used payments. [WORPT](https://www.worpt.com/) · [Report of 13 November](https://paymentexpert.com/2026/11/13/thailand-tests-crypto-to-baht-worpt/)

**DTCC plans the next step for securities on Solana.** On **12 November**, the US financial infrastructure provider announced plans to make eligible assets held at its DTC subsidiary available on Solana in the **second half of 2027**. The broader tokenisation service launched on 28 October; the Solana connection is the planned next step. A named user, a concrete task and a timeframe make the announcement testable. [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/press-releases/2026/dtcc-tokenization-service-to-connect-with-solana)

That is what your Dude likes about these stories: we can judge them by something other than the next green candle. **A bill paid, a transfer that works, or a security that is actually settled.** It was a bitter week in crypto markets. Yet the question of what this technology is useful for gained a few more concrete answers.
